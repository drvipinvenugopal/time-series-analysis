3.1 The Box-Jenkins Revolution and Volatility Clusters

In 1970, George Box and Gwilym Jenkins transformed forecasting from ad-hoc smoothing heuristics into a disciplined three-stage scientific loop: Identification \(\to\) Estimation \(\to\) Diagnostic Checking [3]. By combining difference operators \(\nabla^d = (1-B)^d\) with mixed ARMA polynomials, their ARIMA framework could tame drifting economic indices, industrial sensors, and seasonal demand with just a handful of parameters.

Yet even as ARIMA conquered mean forecasting, a blind spot remained: constant variance (homoscedasticity). In financial markets, daily stock returns are nearly uncorrelated in their direction (\(\rho(h) \approx 0\)), yet large price swings—positive or negative—arrive in tight clusters, followed by calm stretches. In 1982, Robert Engle solved this puzzle by letting the conditional variance \(\sigma_t^2\) evolve autoregressively as a function of past squared shocks (ARCH) [5], and Tim Bollerslev (1986) generalized it into GARCH [6]—forming the backbone of modern Value-at-Risk and portfolio risk management.

3.2 Mixed ARMA(\(p,q\)) Models

When a pure AR(\(p\)) or pure MA(\(q\)) model requires too many parameters to capture both persistent feedback and finite shock memory, combining them yields a parsimonious ARMA(\(p,q\)) model [1], [3].

3.2.1 Key Definitions & Important Terms

Definition 2.1: ARMA(\(p,q\)) Process

A weakly stationary process \(\{X_t\}\) is ARMA(\(p,q\)) if it satisfies: \[\Phi(B) X_t = \Theta(B) Z_t, \qquad Z_t \sim WN(0, \sigma^2) \tag{2.1}\] where \(\Phi(B) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^p \phi_i B^i\) and \(\Theta(B) = 1 + \sum_{j=1}^q \theta_j B^j\).

  • Parameter Redundancy: When \(\Phi(z)\) and \(\Theta(z)\) share a common factor \((1 - c z)\), that factor cancels out, leaving a lower-order model with non-identifiable parameters.
  • Causality & Invertibility: Requires no common roots between \(\Phi(z)\) and \(\Theta(z)\), and all roots of both polynomials to lie strictly outside the unit circle (\(|z| > 1\)).

3.2.2 First-Principles Derivation: \(\psi\)-Weights of ARMA(1,1)

Consider \((1 - \phi B) X_t = (1 + \theta B) Z_t\) with \(|\phi| < 1\). Writing \(X_t = \Psi(B) Z_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \psi_j Z_{t-j}\), we equate coefficients in \(\Phi(B)\Psi(B) = \Theta(B)\): \[(1 - \phi B)(\psi_0 + \psi_1 B + \psi_2 B^2 + \dots) = 1 + \theta B \tag{2.2}\]

  • \(B^0\): \(\psi_0 = 1\).
  • \(B^1\): \(\psi_1 - \phi\psi_0 = \theta \implies \psi_1 = \phi + \theta\).
  • \(B^j\) (\(j \ge 2\)): \(\psi_j - \phi\psi_{j-1} = 0 \implies \psi_j = \phi^{j-1}(\phi + \theta)\).

3.2.3 Sample Problem 2.1 (ARMA(1,1) \(\psi\)-Weights & Variance)

Statement: For \(X_t - 0.4 X_{t-1} = Z_t + 0.8 Z_{t-1}\) with \(Z_t \sim WN(0, 2.0)\), verify causality/invertibility, find \(\psi_0, \psi_1, \psi_2\), and compute \(\gamma(0)\).

Solution: Roots: \(z_\Phi = 2.5\) and \(z_\Theta = -1.25\) (both \(|z| > 1\), no common root \(\implies\) causal & invertible). \(\psi_0 = 1\), \(\psi_1 = 0.4 + 0.8 = 1.2\), \(\psi_2 = 0.4(1.2) = 0.48\). Using \(\gamma(0) = \sigma^2 \sum_{j=0}^\infty \psi_j^2\): \[\gamma(0) = 2.0 \left(1 + \frac{(1.2)^2}{1 - 0.4^2}\right) = 2.0 \left(1 + \frac{1.44}{0.84}\right) = 2.0(1 + 1.7143) \approx 5.4286\]

Key Takeaway:

ARMA(\(p,q\)) models achieve parsimony by blending AR feedback and MA shock memory. Always check that \(\Phi(z)\) and \(\Theta(z)\) share no common roots before estimation.

3.3 Non-Stationarity, Differencing, and ARIMA / SARIMA

Most raw asset prices, EEG baseline drifts, and macroeconomic series wander without a fixed mean. Differencing removes stochastic trends [2], [3].

3.3.1 Key Definitions & Important Terms

  • Deterministic Trend: \(X_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + Y_t\) (stationary fluctuations around a fixed line; shocks are temporary).
  • Stochastic Trend (Unit Root): \(X_t = c + X_{t-1} + Z_t\) (shocks accumulate permanently; \(\mathrm{Var}(X_t | X_0) = t\sigma^2\)).
  • ARIMA(\(p,d,q\)): \(\Phi(B)(1 - B)^d X_t = \Theta(B) Z_t\), meaning \(W_t = \nabla^d X_t\) is a stationary ARMA(\(p,q\)) process.
  • Seasonal ARIMA (\(\mathrm{SARIMA}(p,d,q)\times(P,D,Q)_s\)): \[\Phi_p(B)\tilde{\Phi}_P(B^s)\nabla^d \nabla_s^D X_t = \Theta_q(B)\tilde{\Theta}_Q(B^s) Z_t \tag{2.3}\] where \(\nabla_s X_t = (1 - B^s) X_t = X_t - X_{t-s}\) removes repeating cycles of period \(s\).

3.3.2 First-Principles Result: The Hazard of Over-Differencing

Suppose \(X_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + Z_t\) is already trend-stationary. Applying \(\nabla = 1 - B\) gives: \[\nabla X_t = \beta_1 + Z_t - Z_{t-1} = \beta_1 + (1 - B)Z_t \tag{2.4}\] Differencing turned pure white noise into a non-invertible MA(1) process with \(\theta = -1\) (root \(z = 1\) on the unit circle) and doubled the innovation variance from \(\sigma^2\) to \(2\sigma^2\).

3.3.3 Sample Problem 2.2 (Expanding the Box-Jenkins Airline Model)

Statement: Expand the canonical \(\mathrm{SARIMA}(0,1,1)\times(0,1,1)_{12}\) equation \((1-B)(1-B^{12})X_t = (1 + \theta B)(1 + \tilde{\Theta} B^{12})Z_t\) into a one-step forecast formula for \(\hat{X}_{t+1|t}\).

Solution: Expanding LHS: \(X_t - X_{t-1} - X_{t-12} + X_{t-13}\). Expanding RHS: \(Z_t + \theta Z_{t-1} + \tilde{\Theta} Z_{t-12} + \theta\tilde{\Theta} Z_{t-13}\). Shifting \(t \to t+1\) and taking \(\mathbb{E}[\cdot | \mathcal{F}_t]\) (noting \(\mathbb{E}[Z_{t+1}|\mathcal{F}_t] = 0\)): \[\hat{X}_{t+1|t} = X_t + X_{t-11} - X_{t-12} + \theta \hat{z}_t + \tilde{\Theta} \hat{z}_{t-11} + \theta\tilde{\Theta} \hat{z}_{t-12}\]

Key Takeaway:

Use differencing (\(\nabla^d, \nabla_s^D\)) to eliminate stochastic unit roots and seasonal cycles, but stop as soon as stationarity is achieved to avoid injecting artificial unit-root MA(\(-1\)) terms.

3.4 Multivariate Systems: Vector Autoregression (VAR(\(p\)))

In both multi-channel EEG (frontal, parietal, occipital electrodes) and multi-asset portfolios (equities, bonds, FX), variables co-evolve and lead or lag one another [2], [4].

3.4.1 Key Definitions & Important Terms

Definition 2.2: Vector Autoregressive Model VAR(\(p\))

Let \(\mathbf{Y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^K\). A \(\mathrm{VAR}(p)\) process satisfies: \[\mathbf{Y}_t = \mathbf{c} + \mathbf{\Phi}_1 \mathbf{Y}_{t-1} + \dots + \mathbf{\Phi}_p \mathbf{Y}_{t-p} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t^T] = \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \tag{2.5}\]

  • Granger Causality: \(Y_j\) Granger-causes \(Y_i\) if past lags of \(Y_j\) improve forecasts of \(Y_{i,t}\) beyond \(Y_i\)’s own past (tested via joint Wald \(F\)-test on \(\Phi_{1,ij} = \dots = \Phi_{p,ij} = 0\)).
  • Impulse Response Function (IRF): Traces how a 1-unit orthogonalized shock in variable \(j\) propagates through all \(K\) variables over future steps \(h = 0, 1, 2, \dots\).

3.4.2 First-Principles Result: Companion Matrix Stability Condition

Any VAR(\(p\)) can be stacked into a \(Kp\)-dimensional VAR(1) companion system \(\mathbf{Z}_t = \mathbf{C} + \mathbf{F}\mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + \mathbf{U}_t\). The system is weakly stationary if and only if all eigenvalues of the companion matrix \(\mathbf{F}\) have modulus strictly less than \(1\), equivalently: \[\det\left(\mathbf{I}_K - \sum_{i=1}^p \mathbf{\Phi}_i z^i\right) \neq 0, \quad \forall |z| \le 1 \tag{2.6}\]

3.4.3 Sample Problem 2.3 (Bivariate VAR(1) Stability & Forecasting)

Statement: Let \(\mathbf{Y}_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.1 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_{t-1} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\). Check stability and compute \(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{T+1|T}\) given \(\mathbf{Y}_T = [40, 100]^T\).

Solution: Eigenvalues satisfy \(\det(\mathbf{\Phi}_1 - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = (0.6-\lambda)(0.7-\lambda) - 0.02 = \lambda^2 - 1.3\lambda + 0.40 = (\lambda-0.8)(\lambda-0.5) = 0\). Since \(|\lambda_1|=0.8 < 1\) and \(|\lambda_2|=0.5 < 1\), the system is stable. Forecast: \(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{T+1|T} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6(40)+0.2(100) \\ 0.1(40)+0.7(100) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 44 \\ 74 \end{bmatrix}\).

Key Takeaway:

VAR(\(p\)) models capture bidirectional cross-channel feedback (such as brain-region connectivity or cross-asset spillovers) and enable formal Granger causality testing.

3.5 Conditional Heteroscedastic Models: ARCH and GARCH

Let \(r_t = \mu_t + a_t\) where \(a_t = \sigma_t \epsilon_t\), \(\epsilon_t \overset{i.i.d.}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), and \(\sigma_t^2 = \mathrm{Var}(r_t | \mathcal{F}_{t-1})\) is the time-varying conditional variance [1], [5], [6].

3.5.1 Key Definitions & Important Terms

  • ARCH(\(q\)) (Engle, 1982): \(\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i a_{t-i}^2\) (\(\alpha_0 > 0, \alpha_i \ge 0\)).
  • GARCH(\(p,q\)) (Bollerslev, 1986): \(\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i a_{t-i}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2\) (\(\omega > 0, \alpha_i, \beta_j \ge 0\)).
  • Volatility Persistence (\(\alpha + \beta\)): Governs how slowly a volatility spike decays back to the long-run variance \(\bar{\sigma}^2\).

3.5.2 First-Principles Derivations: ARCH(1) Kurtosis & GARCH(1,1) Persistence

3.5.2.1 Theorem 2.1: Unconditional Variance & Leptokurtosis of ARCH(1)

For \(\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2\) with \(0 \le \alpha_1 < 1\) and \(3\alpha_1^2 < 1\): \[\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1 - \alpha_1}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{\mathbb{E}[a_t^4]}{(\mathbb{E}[a_t^2])^2} = 3\frac{1 - \alpha_1^2}{1 - 3\alpha_1^2} > 3 \tag{2.7}\]

Derivation from First Principles: By the Law of Total Expectation, \(\mathbb{E}[a_t^2] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^2 \mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t^2|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^2] = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1\mathbb{E}[a_{t-1}^2]\). Under stationarity (\(\mathbb{E}[a_t^2] = \bar{\sigma}^2\)), solving gives \(\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1 - \alpha_1}\). For the fourth moment, since \(\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t^4] = 3\): \[\mathbb{E}[a_t^4] = 3\mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^4] = 3\mathbb{E}\big[(\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2)^2\big] = 3\alpha_0^2 + 6\alpha_0\alpha_1 \bar{\sigma}^2 + 3\alpha_1^2 \mathbb{E}[a_{t-1}^4]\] Substituting \(\bar{\sigma}^2 = \alpha_0/(1-\alpha_1)\) and solving for \(\mathbb{E}[a_t^4]\) yields \(\mathbb{E}[a_t^4] = \frac{3\alpha_0^2(1+\alpha_1)}{(1-\alpha_1)(1-3\alpha_1^2)}\). Dividing by \((\bar{\sigma}^2)^2 = \frac{\alpha_0^2}{(1-\alpha_1)^2}\) gives \(\kappa = 3\frac{1 - \alpha_1^2}{1 - 3\alpha_1^2} > 3\). Even with Normal innovations \(\epsilon_t\), ARCH generates fat tails!

3.5.2.2 Theorem 2.2: GARCH(1,1) Stationarity & Multi-Step Variance Forecast

For \(\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2\), taking expectations gives \(\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\omega}{1 - (\alpha + \beta)}\) (requiring \(\alpha + \beta < 1\)). Iterating \(k\) steps ahead: \[\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{t+k}^2 | \mathcal{F}_t] = \bar{\sigma}^2 + (\alpha + \beta)^{k-1}\big(\sigma_{t+1}^2 - \bar{\sigma}^2\big) \tag{2.8}\]

3.5.3 Sample Problem 2.4 (ARCH(1) Risk & Kurtosis)

Statement: A stock return series follows ARCH(1): \(\sigma_t^2 = 0.00004 + 0.35 a_{t-1}^2\). Compute \(\bar{\sigma}^2\), annualized volatility (\(\times\sqrt{252}\)), and theoretical kurtosis \(\kappa\).

Solution: \(\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{0.00004}{1 - 0.35} \approx 6.1538 \times 10^{-5}\). Annualized volatility: \(\sqrt{252 \times 6.1538\times 10^{-5}} \approx 12.45\%\). Since \(3(0.35)^2 = 0.3675 < 1\), \(\kappa = 3\frac{1 - 0.1225}{1 - 0.3675} \approx 4.162 > 3\) (leptokurtic).

Key Takeaway:

ARCH and GARCH model time-varying risk (\(\sigma_t^2\)), naturally explaining both volatility clustering and fat-tailed return distributions when \(\alpha + \beta < 1\).

3.6 3-Tier Graduated Problem Ladder

3.6.1 Tier 1: By-Hand Intuition

  • Problem 2.5 (Practice): Identify the orders \((p,d,q)\times(P,D,Q)_{12}\) of \((1 - 0.5B)(1 - B)(1 - B^{12})X_t = (1 + 0.3B^{12})Z_t\). (Answer: \(\mathrm{SARIMA}(1,1,0)\times(0,1,1)_{12}\).)
  • Problem 2.6 (Practice): A GARCH(1,1) has \(\omega = 2\times 10^{-6}\), \(\alpha = 0.09\), \(\beta = 0.89\). Compute the long-run daily volatility \(\bar{\sigma}\). (Answer: \(\bar{\sigma}^2 = 2\times 10^{-6}/0.02 = 0.0001 \implies \bar{\sigma} = 1.0\%\).)

3.6.2 Tier 2: Analytical & Exam-Style

  • Problem 2.7 (Worked — GARCH(1,1) as ARMA(1,1)): Show that \(a_t^2\) in GARCH(1,1) obeys an ARMA(1,1) equation. Solution: Let \(\nu_t = a_t^2 - \sigma_t^2\) (note \(\mathbb{E}[\nu_t|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0\)). Substituting \(\sigma_t^2 = a_t^2 - \nu_t\) into \(\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2\) gives \(a_t^2 - \nu_t = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta(a_{t-1}^2 - \nu_{t-1}) \implies a_t^2 = \omega + (\alpha + \beta)a_{t-1}^2 + \nu_t - \beta\nu_{t-1}\), which is ARMA(1,1) with \(\phi_1 = \alpha+\beta\) and \(\theta_1 = -\beta\).

3.6.3 Tier 3: Domain Case Studies (EEG & Finance/Portfolio)

  • Problem 2.8 (EEG Multi-Channel Granger Coupling — Worked): In a 2-channel EEG VAR(1) connecting Frontal (\(Y_{1,t}\)) and Occipital (\(Y_{2,t}\)) cortex, \(\mathbf{\Phi}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.0 \\ 0.35 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\). Does Occipital activity Granger-cause Frontal activity? Solution: Look at row 1, col 2: \(\Phi_{1,12} = 0.0\), so Occipital lags do not predict Frontal activity. However, \(\Phi_{1,21} = 0.35 \neq 0\), meaning Frontal activity unidirectionally Granger-causes Occipital activity.
  • Problem 2.9 (Dynamic Portfolio Value-at-Risk — Practice): Using Problem 2.6’s GARCH(1,1), today’s shock is \(a_T = -0.03\) (\(-3\%\)) and \(\sigma_T = 0.01\). Compute tomorrow’s 1-day 99% VaR (\(\text{VaR}_{0.99} = 2.326\,\sigma_{T+1}\)) for a Rs.10,00,000 portfolio. (Answer: \(\sigma_{T+1}^2 = 2\times 10^{-6} + 0.09(0.0009) + 0.89(0.0001) = 0.000172 \implies \sigma_{T+1} = 1.311\%\), \(\text{VaR}_{0.99} \approx 30,505\).)

3.7 Dual-Track Laboratory Experimental Section

3.7.1 Lab Track A: Multi-Channel EEG VAR(p) & Granger Connectivity

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from statsmodels.tsa.api import VAR

# Simulate coupled 2-channel EEG (Frontal -> Occipital delayed propagation)
np.random.seed(10)
n = 600
frontal = np.zeros(n)
occipital = np.zeros(n)
e1, e2 = np.random.normal(0, 1, n), np.random.normal(0, 1, n)

for t in range(1, n):
    frontal[t] = 0.65 * frontal[t-1] + e1[t]
    occipital[t] = 0.45 * occipital[t-1] + 0.40 * frontal[t-1] + e2[t]

eeg_df = pd.DataFrame({"Frontal": frontal, "Occipital": occipital})
var_fit = VAR(eeg_df).fit(maxlags=2, ic="aic")
print(var_fit.summary())

# Test Granger causality: Does Frontal Granger-cause Occipital?
gc_f_to_o = var_fit.test_causality("Occipital", ["Frontal"], kind="f")
gc_o_to_f = var_fit.test_causality("Frontal", ["Occipital"], kind="f")
print(f"\nFrontal -> Occipital p-value: {gc_f_to_o.pvalue:.4e}")
print(f"Occipital -> Frontal p-value: {gc_o_to_f.pvalue:.4e}")

3.7.2 Lab Track B: SARIMA Forecasting & GARCH(1,1) Portfolio VaR

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax import SARIMAX
from scipy.optimize import minimize

# Part 1: Box-Jenkins Airline SARIMA(0,1,1)x(0,1,1)_12
air = sm.datasets.get_rdataset("AirPassengers", package="datasets").data["value"]
log_air = np.log(pd.Series(air.values, index=pd.date_range("1949-01-01", periods=len(air), freq="MS")))
sarima = SARIMAX(log_air.iloc[:-12], order=(0, 1, 1), seasonal_order=(0, 1, 1, 12)).fit(disp=False)
pred = np.exp(sarima.get_forecast(12).predicted_mean)
rmse = np.sqrt(np.mean((np.exp(log_air.iloc[-12:]) - pred)**2))
print(f"Airline 12-Month Out-of-Sample RMSE: {rmse:.2f} thousand passengers")

# Part 2: GARCH(1,1) MLE & 99% VaR Backtest on Equity Returns
np.random.seed(42)
T = 1000
r, s2 = np.zeros(T), np.zeros(T)
s2[0] = 0.0001
eps = np.random.normal(0, 1, T)
for t in range(1, T):
    s2[t] = 0.00001 + 0.08 * r[t-1]**2 + 0.88 * s2[t-1]
    r[t] = np.sqrt(s2[t]) * eps[t]

def neg_ll(params):
    w, a, b = params
    if w <= 0 or a < 0 or b < 0 or a + b >= 1:
        return 1e10
    sig2 = np.empty(T)
    sig2[0] = np.var(r)
    for t in range(1, T):
        sig2[t] = w + a * r[t-1]**2 + b * sig2[t-1]
    return 0.5 * np.sum(np.log(2 * np.pi * sig2) + r**2 / sig2)

res = minimize(neg_ll, [0.00002, 0.1, 0.8], method="Nelder-Mead")
w_hat, a_hat, b_hat = res.x
print(f"GARCH(1,1) MLE -> omega={w_hat:.6f}, alpha={a_hat:.4f}, beta={b_hat:.4f}, persistence={a_hat+b_hat:.4f}")

3.8 Summary of Concepts

  • ARMA(\(p,q\)): Combines AR and MA polynomials; valid identification requires no common factors and all roots outside \(|z| > 1\).
  • ARIMA & SARIMA: First and seasonal differencing (\(\nabla^d \nabla_s^D\)) eliminate unit roots and periodic trends without over-differencing.
  • VAR(\(p\)): Models multi-channel/multi-asset feedback; stability requires companion eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle, enabling Granger causality and IRF analysis.
  • ARCH / GARCH: Models time-varying conditional variance \(\sigma_t^2\), producing volatility clustering, fat tails (\(\kappa > 3\)), and dynamic Value-at-Risk bounds.

## The Box-Jenkins Revolution and Volatility Clusters

In 1970, George Box and Gwilym Jenkins transformed forecasting from ad-hoc smoothing heuristics into a disciplined three-stage scientific loop: **Identification $\to$ Estimation $\to$ Diagnostic Checking** [@box2016time]. By combining difference operators $\nabla^d = (1-B)^d$ with mixed ARMA polynomials, their ARIMA framework could tame drifting economic indices, industrial sensors, and seasonal demand with just a handful of parameters.

Yet even as ARIMA conquered mean forecasting, a blind spot remained: **constant variance (homoscedasticity)**. In financial markets, daily stock returns are nearly uncorrelated in their *direction* ($\rho(h) \approx 0$), yet large price swings—positive or negative—arrive in tight clusters, followed by calm stretches. In 1982, Robert Engle solved this puzzle by letting the *conditional variance* $\sigma_t^2$ evolve autoregressively as a function of past squared shocks (**ARCH**) [@engle1982autoregressive], and Tim Bollerslev (1986) generalized it into **GARCH** [@bollerslev1986generalized]—forming the backbone of modern Value-at-Risk and portfolio risk management.



## Mixed ARMA($p,q$) Models

When a pure AR($p$) or pure MA($q$) model requires too many parameters to capture both persistent feedback and finite shock memory, combining them yields a parsimonious **ARMA($p,q$)** model [@box2016time; @cryer2008time].

### Key Definitions & Important Terms

::: {.callout-note icon="false"}
#### Definition 2.1: ARMA($p,q$) Process
A weakly stationary process $\{X_t\}$ is **ARMA($p,q$)** if it satisfies:
$$\Phi(B) X_t = \Theta(B) Z_t, \qquad Z_t \sim WN(0, \sigma^2) \tag{2.1}$$
where $\Phi(B) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^p \phi_i B^i$ and $\Theta(B) = 1 + \sum_{j=1}^q \theta_j B^j$.
:::

* **Parameter Redundancy**: When $\Phi(z)$ and $\Theta(z)$ share a common factor $(1 - c z)$, that factor cancels out, leaving a lower-order model with non-identifiable parameters.
* **Causality & Invertibility**: Requires no common roots between $\Phi(z)$ and $\Theta(z)$, and all roots of both polynomials to lie strictly outside the unit circle ($|z| > 1$).

### First-Principles Derivation: $\psi$-Weights of ARMA(1,1)

Consider $(1 - \phi B) X_t = (1 + \theta B) Z_t$ with $|\phi| < 1$. Writing $X_t = \Psi(B) Z_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \psi_j Z_{t-j}$, we equate coefficients in $\Phi(B)\Psi(B) = \Theta(B)$:
$$(1 - \phi B)(\psi_0 + \psi_1 B + \psi_2 B^2 + \dots) = 1 + \theta B \tag{2.2}$$

- $B^0$: $\psi_0 = 1$.
- $B^1$: $\psi_1 - \phi\psi_0 = \theta \implies \psi_1 = \phi + \theta$.
- $B^j$ ($j \ge 2$): $\psi_j - \phi\psi_{j-1} = 0 \implies \psi_j = \phi^{j-1}(\phi + \theta)$.

### Sample Problem 2.1 (ARMA(1,1) $\psi$-Weights & Variance)
**Statement**: For $X_t - 0.4 X_{t-1} = Z_t + 0.8 Z_{t-1}$ with $Z_t \sim WN(0, 2.0)$, verify causality/invertibility, find $\psi_0, \psi_1, \psi_2$, and compute $\gamma(0)$.

**Solution**:
Roots: $z_\Phi = 2.5$ and $z_\Theta = -1.25$ (both $|z| > 1$, no common root $\implies$ causal & invertible).
$\psi_0 = 1$, $\psi_1 = 0.4 + 0.8 = 1.2$, $\psi_2 = 0.4(1.2) = 0.48$.
Using $\gamma(0) = \sigma^2 \sum_{j=0}^\infty \psi_j^2$:
$$\gamma(0) = 2.0 \left(1 + \frac{(1.2)^2}{1 - 0.4^2}\right) = 2.0 \left(1 + \frac{1.44}{0.84}\right) = 2.0(1 + 1.7143) \approx 5.4286$$

::: {.callout-tip}
#### Key Takeaway:
ARMA($p,q$) models achieve parsimony by blending AR feedback and MA shock memory. Always check that $\Phi(z)$ and $\Theta(z)$ share no common roots before estimation.
:::



## Non-Stationarity, Differencing, and ARIMA / SARIMA

Most raw asset prices, EEG baseline drifts, and macroeconomic series wander without a fixed mean. Differencing removes stochastic trends [@box2016time; @shumway2011time].

### Key Definitions & Important Terms

* **Deterministic Trend**: $X_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + Y_t$ (stationary fluctuations around a fixed line; shocks are temporary).
* **Stochastic Trend (Unit Root)**: $X_t = c + X_{t-1} + Z_t$ (shocks accumulate permanently; $\mathrm{Var}(X_t | X_0) = t\sigma^2$).
* **ARIMA($p,d,q$)**: $\Phi(B)(1 - B)^d X_t = \Theta(B) Z_t$, meaning $W_t = \nabla^d X_t$ is a stationary ARMA($p,q$) process.
* **Seasonal ARIMA ($\mathrm{SARIMA}(p,d,q)\times(P,D,Q)_s$)**:
  $$\Phi_p(B)\tilde{\Phi}_P(B^s)\nabla^d \nabla_s^D X_t = \Theta_q(B)\tilde{\Theta}_Q(B^s) Z_t \tag{2.3}$$
  where $\nabla_s X_t = (1 - B^s) X_t = X_t - X_{t-s}$ removes repeating cycles of period $s$.

### First-Principles Result: The Hazard of Over-Differencing

Suppose $X_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + Z_t$ is already trend-stationary. Applying $\nabla = 1 - B$ gives:
$$\nabla X_t = \beta_1 + Z_t - Z_{t-1} = \beta_1 + (1 - B)Z_t \tag{2.4}$$
Differencing turned pure white noise into a **non-invertible MA(1)** process with $\theta = -1$ (root $z = 1$ on the unit circle) and doubled the innovation variance from $\sigma^2$ to $2\sigma^2$.

### Sample Problem 2.2 (Expanding the Box-Jenkins Airline Model)
**Statement**: Expand the canonical $\mathrm{SARIMA}(0,1,1)\times(0,1,1)_{12}$ equation $(1-B)(1-B^{12})X_t = (1 + \theta B)(1 + \tilde{\Theta} B^{12})Z_t$ into a one-step forecast formula for $\hat{X}_{t+1|t}$.

**Solution**:
Expanding LHS: $X_t - X_{t-1} - X_{t-12} + X_{t-13}$.
Expanding RHS: $Z_t + \theta Z_{t-1} + \tilde{\Theta} Z_{t-12} + \theta\tilde{\Theta} Z_{t-13}$.
Shifting $t \to t+1$ and taking $\mathbb{E}[\cdot | \mathcal{F}_t]$ (noting $\mathbb{E}[Z_{t+1}|\mathcal{F}_t] = 0$):
$$\hat{X}_{t+1|t} = X_t + X_{t-11} - X_{t-12} + \theta \hat{z}_t + \tilde{\Theta} \hat{z}_{t-11} + \theta\tilde{\Theta} \hat{z}_{t-12}$$

::: {.callout-tip}
#### Key Takeaway:
Use differencing ($\nabla^d, \nabla_s^D$) to eliminate stochastic unit roots and seasonal cycles, but stop as soon as stationarity is achieved to avoid injecting artificial unit-root MA($-1$) terms.
:::



## Multivariate Systems: Vector Autoregression (VAR($p$))

In both **multi-channel EEG** (frontal, parietal, occipital electrodes) and **multi-asset portfolios** (equities, bonds, FX), variables co-evolve and lead or lag one another [@shumway2011time; @nielsen2019practical].

### Key Definitions & Important Terms

::: {.callout-note icon="false"}
#### Definition 2.2: Vector Autoregressive Model VAR($p$)
Let $\mathbf{Y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^K$. A **$\mathrm{VAR}(p)$** process satisfies:
$$\mathbf{Y}_t = \mathbf{c} + \mathbf{\Phi}_1 \mathbf{Y}_{t-1} + \dots + \mathbf{\Phi}_p \mathbf{Y}_{t-p} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t^T] = \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \tag{2.5}$$
:::

* **Granger Causality**: $Y_j$ *Granger-causes* $Y_i$ if past lags of $Y_j$ improve forecasts of $Y_{i,t}$ beyond $Y_i$'s own past (tested via joint Wald $F$-test on $\Phi_{1,ij} = \dots = \Phi_{p,ij} = 0$).
* **Impulse Response Function (IRF)**: Traces how a 1-unit orthogonalized shock in variable $j$ propagates through all $K$ variables over future steps $h = 0, 1, 2, \dots$.

### First-Principles Result: Companion Matrix Stability Condition
Any VAR($p$) can be stacked into a $Kp$-dimensional VAR(1) companion system $\mathbf{Z}_t = \mathbf{C} + \mathbf{F}\mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + \mathbf{U}_t$. The system is weakly stationary if and only if all eigenvalues of the companion matrix $\mathbf{F}$ have modulus strictly less than $1$, equivalently:
$$\det\left(\mathbf{I}_K - \sum_{i=1}^p \mathbf{\Phi}_i z^i\right) \neq 0, \quad \forall |z| \le 1 \tag{2.6}$$

### Sample Problem 2.3 (Bivariate VAR(1) Stability & Forecasting)
**Statement**: Let $\mathbf{Y}_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.1 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_{t-1} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$. Check stability and compute $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{T+1|T}$ given $\mathbf{Y}_T = [40, 100]^T$.

**Solution**:
Eigenvalues satisfy $\det(\mathbf{\Phi}_1 - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = (0.6-\lambda)(0.7-\lambda) - 0.02 = \lambda^2 - 1.3\lambda + 0.40 = (\lambda-0.8)(\lambda-0.5) = 0$. Since $|\lambda_1|=0.8 < 1$ and $|\lambda_2|=0.5 < 1$, the system is **stable**.
Forecast: $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{T+1|T} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6(40)+0.2(100) \\ 0.1(40)+0.7(100) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 44 \\ 74 \end{bmatrix}$.

::: {.callout-tip}
#### Key Takeaway:
VAR($p$) models capture bidirectional cross-channel feedback (such as brain-region connectivity or cross-asset spillovers) and enable formal Granger causality testing.
:::

## Conditional Heteroscedastic Models: ARCH and GARCH

Let $r_t = \mu_t + a_t$ where $a_t = \sigma_t \epsilon_t$, $\epsilon_t \overset{i.i.d.}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, and $\sigma_t^2 = \mathrm{Var}(r_t | \mathcal{F}_{t-1})$ is the time-varying conditional variance [@cryer2008time; @engle1982autoregressive; @bollerslev1986generalized].

### Key Definitions & Important Terms

* **ARCH($q$) (Engle, 1982)**: $\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i a_{t-i}^2$ ($\alpha_0 > 0, \alpha_i \ge 0$).
* **GARCH($p,q$) (Bollerslev, 1986)**: $\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i a_{t-i}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2$ ($\omega > 0, \alpha_i, \beta_j \ge 0$).
* **Volatility Persistence ($\alpha + \beta$)**: Governs how slowly a volatility spike decays back to the long-run variance $\bar{\sigma}^2$.

### First-Principles Derivations: ARCH(1) Kurtosis & GARCH(1,1) Persistence

#### Theorem 2.1: Unconditional Variance & Leptokurtosis of ARCH(1)
For $\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2$ with $0 \le \alpha_1 < 1$ and $3\alpha_1^2 < 1$:
$$\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1 - \alpha_1}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{\mathbb{E}[a_t^4]}{(\mathbb{E}[a_t^2])^2} = 3\frac{1 - \alpha_1^2}{1 - 3\alpha_1^2} > 3 \tag{2.7}$$

**Derivation from First Principles**:
By the Law of Total Expectation, $\mathbb{E}[a_t^2] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^2 \mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t^2|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^2] = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1\mathbb{E}[a_{t-1}^2]$. Under stationarity ($\mathbb{E}[a_t^2] = \bar{\sigma}^2$), solving gives $\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1 - \alpha_1}$.
For the fourth moment, since $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t^4] = 3$:
$$\mathbb{E}[a_t^4] = 3\mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^4] = 3\mathbb{E}\big[(\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2)^2\big] = 3\alpha_0^2 + 6\alpha_0\alpha_1 \bar{\sigma}^2 + 3\alpha_1^2 \mathbb{E}[a_{t-1}^4]$$
Substituting $\bar{\sigma}^2 = \alpha_0/(1-\alpha_1)$ and solving for $\mathbb{E}[a_t^4]$ yields $\mathbb{E}[a_t^4] = \frac{3\alpha_0^2(1+\alpha_1)}{(1-\alpha_1)(1-3\alpha_1^2)}$. Dividing by $(\bar{\sigma}^2)^2 = \frac{\alpha_0^2}{(1-\alpha_1)^2}$ gives $\kappa = 3\frac{1 - \alpha_1^2}{1 - 3\alpha_1^2} > 3$. Even with Normal innovations $\epsilon_t$, ARCH generates **fat tails**!

#### Theorem 2.2: GARCH(1,1) Stationarity & Multi-Step Variance Forecast
For $\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2$, taking expectations gives $\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\omega}{1 - (\alpha + \beta)}$ (requiring $\alpha + \beta < 1$). Iterating $k$ steps ahead:
$$\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{t+k}^2 | \mathcal{F}_t] = \bar{\sigma}^2 + (\alpha + \beta)^{k-1}\big(\sigma_{t+1}^2 - \bar{\sigma}^2\big) \tag{2.8}$$

### Sample Problem 2.4 (ARCH(1) Risk & Kurtosis)
**Statement**: A stock return series follows ARCH(1): $\sigma_t^2 = 0.00004 + 0.35 a_{t-1}^2$. Compute $\bar{\sigma}^2$, annualized volatility ($\times\sqrt{252}$), and theoretical kurtosis $\kappa$.

**Solution**:
$\bar{\sigma}^2 = \frac{0.00004}{1 - 0.35} \approx 6.1538 \times 10^{-5}$. Annualized volatility: $\sqrt{252 \times 6.1538\times 10^{-5}} \approx 12.45\%$.
Since $3(0.35)^2 = 0.3675 < 1$, $\kappa = 3\frac{1 - 0.1225}{1 - 0.3675} \approx 4.162 > 3$ (leptokurtic).

::: {.callout-tip}
#### Key Takeaway:
ARCH and GARCH model *time-varying risk* ($\sigma_t^2$), naturally explaining both volatility clustering and fat-tailed return distributions when $\alpha + \beta < 1$.
:::


## 3-Tier Graduated Problem Ladder

### Tier 1: By-Hand Intuition
* **Problem 2.5 (Practice)**: Identify the orders $(p,d,q)\times(P,D,Q)_{12}$ of $(1 - 0.5B)(1 - B)(1 - B^{12})X_t = (1 + 0.3B^{12})Z_t$. *(Answer: $\mathrm{SARIMA}(1,1,0)\times(0,1,1)_{12}$.)*
* **Problem 2.6 (Practice)**: A GARCH(1,1) has $\omega = 2\times 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = 0.09$, $\beta = 0.89$. Compute the long-run daily volatility $\bar{\sigma}$. *(Answer: $\bar{\sigma}^2 = 2\times 10^{-6}/0.02 = 0.0001 \implies \bar{\sigma} = 1.0\%$.)*

### Tier 2: Analytical & Exam-Style
* **Problem 2.7 (Worked — GARCH(1,1) as ARMA(1,1))**: Show that $a_t^2$ in GARCH(1,1) obeys an ARMA(1,1) equation.
  *Solution*: Let $\nu_t = a_t^2 - \sigma_t^2$ (note $\mathbb{E}[\nu_t|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0$). Substituting $\sigma_t^2 = a_t^2 - \nu_t$ into $\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2$ gives $a_t^2 - \nu_t = \omega + \alpha a_{t-1}^2 + \beta(a_{t-1}^2 - \nu_{t-1}) \implies a_t^2 = \omega + (\alpha + \beta)a_{t-1}^2 + \nu_t - \beta\nu_{t-1}$, which is ARMA(1,1) with $\phi_1 = \alpha+\beta$ and $\theta_1 = -\beta$.

### Tier 3: Domain Case Studies (EEG & Finance/Portfolio)
* **Problem 2.8 (EEG Multi-Channel Granger Coupling — Worked)**: In a 2-channel EEG VAR(1) connecting Frontal ($Y_{1,t}$) and Occipital ($Y_{2,t}$) cortex, $\mathbf{\Phi}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.0 \\ 0.35 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}$. Does Occipital activity Granger-cause Frontal activity?
  *Solution*: Look at row 1, col 2: $\Phi_{1,12} = 0.0$, so Occipital lags do **not** predict Frontal activity. However, $\Phi_{1,21} = 0.35 \neq 0$, meaning Frontal activity *unidirectionally Granger-causes* Occipital activity.
* **Problem 2.9 (Dynamic Portfolio Value-at-Risk — Practice)**: Using Problem 2.6's GARCH(1,1), today's shock is $a_T = -0.03$ ($-3\%$) and $\sigma_T = 0.01$. Compute tomorrow's 1-day 99% VaR ($\text{VaR}_{0.99} = 2.326\,\sigma_{T+1}$) for a Rs.10,00,000 portfolio. *(Answer: $\sigma_{T+1}^2 = 2\times 10^{-6} + 0.09(0.0009) + 0.89(0.0001) = 0.000172 \implies \sigma_{T+1} = 1.311\%$, $\text{VaR}_{0.99} \approx 30,505$.)*


## Dual-Track Laboratory Experimental Section

### Lab Track A: Multi-Channel EEG VAR(p) & Granger Connectivity

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from statsmodels.tsa.api import VAR

# Simulate coupled 2-channel EEG (Frontal -> Occipital delayed propagation)
np.random.seed(10)
n = 600
frontal = np.zeros(n)
occipital = np.zeros(n)
e1, e2 = np.random.normal(0, 1, n), np.random.normal(0, 1, n)

for t in range(1, n):
    frontal[t] = 0.65 * frontal[t-1] + e1[t]
    occipital[t] = 0.45 * occipital[t-1] + 0.40 * frontal[t-1] + e2[t]

eeg_df = pd.DataFrame({"Frontal": frontal, "Occipital": occipital})
var_fit = VAR(eeg_df).fit(maxlags=2, ic="aic")
print(var_fit.summary())

# Test Granger causality: Does Frontal Granger-cause Occipital?
gc_f_to_o = var_fit.test_causality("Occipital", ["Frontal"], kind="f")
gc_o_to_f = var_fit.test_causality("Frontal", ["Occipital"], kind="f")
print(f"\nFrontal -> Occipital p-value: {gc_f_to_o.pvalue:.4e}")
print(f"Occipital -> Frontal p-value: {gc_o_to_f.pvalue:.4e}")
```

### Lab Track B: SARIMA Forecasting & GARCH(1,1) Portfolio VaR

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax import SARIMAX
from scipy.optimize import minimize

# Part 1: Box-Jenkins Airline SARIMA(0,1,1)x(0,1,1)_12
air = sm.datasets.get_rdataset("AirPassengers", package="datasets").data["value"]
log_air = np.log(pd.Series(air.values, index=pd.date_range("1949-01-01", periods=len(air), freq="MS")))
sarima = SARIMAX(log_air.iloc[:-12], order=(0, 1, 1), seasonal_order=(0, 1, 1, 12)).fit(disp=False)
pred = np.exp(sarima.get_forecast(12).predicted_mean)
rmse = np.sqrt(np.mean((np.exp(log_air.iloc[-12:]) - pred)**2))
print(f"Airline 12-Month Out-of-Sample RMSE: {rmse:.2f} thousand passengers")

# Part 2: GARCH(1,1) MLE & 99% VaR Backtest on Equity Returns
np.random.seed(42)
T = 1000
r, s2 = np.zeros(T), np.zeros(T)
s2[0] = 0.0001
eps = np.random.normal(0, 1, T)
for t in range(1, T):
    s2[t] = 0.00001 + 0.08 * r[t-1]**2 + 0.88 * s2[t-1]
    r[t] = np.sqrt(s2[t]) * eps[t]

def neg_ll(params):
    w, a, b = params
    if w <= 0 or a < 0 or b < 0 or a + b >= 1:
        return 1e10
    sig2 = np.empty(T)
    sig2[0] = np.var(r)
    for t in range(1, T):
        sig2[t] = w + a * r[t-1]**2 + b * sig2[t-1]
    return 0.5 * np.sum(np.log(2 * np.pi * sig2) + r**2 / sig2)

res = minimize(neg_ll, [0.00002, 0.1, 0.8], method="Nelder-Mead")
w_hat, a_hat, b_hat = res.x
print(f"GARCH(1,1) MLE -> omega={w_hat:.6f}, alpha={a_hat:.4f}, beta={b_hat:.4f}, persistence={a_hat+b_hat:.4f}")
```

## Summary of Concepts

* **ARMA($p,q$)**: Combines AR and MA polynomials; valid identification requires no common factors and all roots outside $|z| > 1$.
* **ARIMA & SARIMA**: First and seasonal differencing ($\nabla^d \nabla_s^D$) eliminate unit roots and periodic trends without over-differencing.
* **VAR($p$)**: Models multi-channel/multi-asset feedback; stability requires companion eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle, enabling Granger causality and IRF analysis.
* **ARCH / GARCH**: Models time-varying conditional variance $\sigma_t^2$, producing volatility clustering, fat tails ($\kappa > 3$), and dynamic Value-at-Risk bounds.
